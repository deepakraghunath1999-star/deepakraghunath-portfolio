03Decision lab

Don't take my word for it. Run the models.

Four models I built, running entirely in your browser. You can change every input, the assumptions are written next to each model, and the same inputs always give the same answer.

RetireSim

How long does a retirement portfolio last, and what do spending guardrails buy you? The model runs 5,000 simulated market paths and re-runs every time you change an input.

Guyton-Klinger guardrails

Annual steps, with each withdrawal taken at the start of the year. Returns are lognormal with the mean and volatility you set. The guardrails work like this: skip the inflation raise after a losing year if the withdrawal rate is above where it started; cut spending 10% if the rate is 20% above its start (except in the final 15 years); raise it 10% if the rate is 20% below its start. The random seed is fixed. Illustrative only, not financial advice.

RMD exception monitor

A synthetic version of the logic behind 100% RMD compliance at Ameriprise. It checks each account's age against the rules, applies the IRS divisors and scheduled distributions, and escalates flags as the December 31 deadline gets closer.

Show

Ten fictional accounts. The model applies SECURE 2.0 start ages (73 for people born 1951–59, 75 for 1960 and later) and the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table to prior year-end balances. First-year RMDs can be deferred to April 1 of the following year. A shortfall incurs a 25% excise tax, or 10% if corrected in time. Illustrative only, not tax advice.

Efficient frontier

A long-only mean-variance allocation across four asset classes, with correlations included. It finds the maximum-Sharpe (tangency) portfolio and the minimum-variance portfolio.

Assumed volatilities: US equity 16%, international equity 18%, US bonds 6%, REITs 19%. Equity–equity correlation is 0.80, equity–REIT 0.60–0.65, and bond–REIT 0.25. Results come from 6,000 sampled long-only portfolios plus the four single-asset corners. These are illustrative assumptions, not capital-market forecasts.
